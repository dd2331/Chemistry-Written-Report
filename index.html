1Purpose

Why we ran this investigation.

A hydroponic farm needs a nutrient-research solution. Our team prepared a 0.100 mol/dm³ stock solution of potassium nitrate (KNO3), diluted it to 0.0200 mol/dm³, and quality-checked both so the farm can deliver an accurate nutrient concentration to its plants.

2Calculations

Dilution of the stock solution using C1V1 = C2V2.

Given values

  • Initial concentration (C1): 0.100 mol/dm³
  • Diluted concentration (C2): 0.0200 mol/dm³
  • Final volume (V2): 100.0 cm³

Volume of stock needed

C1 × V1 = C2 × V2
0.100 × V1 = 0.0200 × 100.0
0.100 × V1 = 2.00
V1 = 2.00 ÷ 0.100
V1 = 20.0 cm³

So 20.0 cm³ of stock is transferred to a 100.0 cm³ volumetric flask and made up to the mark with distilled water (a 5× dilution).

4Response to the Client's Question

Why concentration control matters on a hydroponic farm.

Excessive nutrient concentrations, such as the high ionic strength produced by salts like KNO3, create a high osmotic pressure in the nutrient solution outside the roots. This prevents roots from absorbing water effectively, which leads to nutrient toxicities that disrupt metabolic processes and can stunt plant growth or cause wilting.

This is why an accurately prepared and accurately diluted solution matters: it keeps the nutrient strength at the level plants can actually use.

5Error Analysis & Corrective Action

Three sources of error, their effect on the result, and how we correct each.

Measurement errors

Error: Misreading the meniscus of the stock solution when transferring can change the final volume and concentration.
Effect: The final diluted solution will not match the target concentration (0.0200 mol/dm³).
Correction: Read the bottom of the meniscus at eye level, and use droppers for accuracy (and to prevent repeating the measurements).

Material loss during transfer

Error: Small amounts of stock solution spilled outside the volumetric flask during transfer.
Effect: Less solute ends up in the final flask, resulting in a lower concentration.
Correction: Rinse as much residual solution as possible with distilled water, and use a funnel to avoid spilling.

Incomplete mixing

Error: Mixing the solution after filling the flask was not thorough.
Effect: The solute is not evenly distributed throughout the solvent, leading to inconsistent concentrations.
Correction: Invert the flask multiple times (with a stopper) so the solution is uniform.

7Recommendation to the Client

What we advise the farm to do.

To optimize crop yield and prevent salt-induced stress at the farm level, operators should strictly follow standardized solution preparation steps. Implementing a mandatory quality-control log before pumping batch solutions into active channels will safeguard plant health while minimizing chemical waste and supporting UAE sustainability targets.

Recommended actions

  1. Standardize preparation: use funnels, complete flask inversions, and eye-level meniscus checks so nutrient delivery is uniform and accurately diluted across all growing channels.
  2. Install EC sensors: place automated Electrical Conductivity (EC) sensors directly in the hydroponic dosing lines to continuously monitor nutrient strength and alert staff before high osmotic pressure causes root damage, toxicity, or wilting.
  3. Keep a quality-control log: make it mandatory to complete the log before any batch solution is pumped into active channels.
